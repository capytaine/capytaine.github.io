capytaine.tools.perturbation_expansion module¶

Truncated perturbation series (“higher-order dual numbers”).

class capytaine.tools.perturbation_expansion.DifferenceFrequencyExpansion(coefficients)[source]¶

Bases: PerturbationExpansion

Order-2 expansion of a field in a sea made of several wave components at one frequency.

For a field of shape S, with W wave components (e.g. wave directions):

  • order 0, shape S: still water value (geometry, hydrostatic pressure, …). Must be real.

  • order 1, shape (W, *S): x1[m] is the complex amplitude of the response to wave m alone.

  • order 2, shape (W, W, *S): x2[m, n] is the difference-frequency QTF: the second-order part of the field is sum_mn x2[m, n] exp(-i (omega_m - omega_n) t). Hermitian in (m, n).

Products keep the difference-frequency part of the product of first-order terms: Q_mn = 1/4 (a_m conj(b_n) + conj(a_n) b_m), such that Q_mm = 1/2 Re(a_m conj(b_m)) is the mean value. A complex factor (e.g. i*omega) must be applied to the first-order amplitudes before building the expansion, never to an expansion that has an order-2 part.

The order-0 and order-2 coefficients can be given as scalars (e.g. 0), and are then broadcast. When two operands have a different number of field axes, size-1 axes are inserted in front of the field axes of the one that has fewer (NumPy broadcasting of the field axes only). The wave axes are leading batch axes and the field axes are at the end: index with x[…, i].

class capytaine.tools.perturbation_expansion.PerturbationExpansion(coefficients)[source]¶

Bases: object

A quantity x0 + eps*x1 + eps^2*x2 + … + eps^N*xN, truncated at order N.

Each coefficient may be a plain scalar or a numpy array (vector, matrix, …). Use .coefficients[k] to get the raw coefficient of a specific order. Two expansions can only be combined if they share the same truncation order N. Plain scalars and arrays used as operands are constants: x stands for x + 0*eps + ….

The axes of the “field” (e.g. (nb_faces, 3) for a vector field) are at the end of the coefficients. Leading axes are batch axes, such as the wave axes of DifferenceFrequencyExpansion. Operations act on each coefficient with the usual NumPy broadcasting rules. Methods taking an axis (sum, expand_dims, squeeze) take a negative axis (axis=-1 for the last one), and trace and T act on the last two axes.
